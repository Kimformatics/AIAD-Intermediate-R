The course

From raw data to a professional Public Health product

John Snow’s historic cholera investigation serves as the continuous case study throughout this four-week applied course. You’ll progress from reproducible data preparation and epidemiologic analysis to spatial exploration, project refinement, GitHub publishing, dashboard development, and a final presentation.

01 / ANALYZE

Work with real questions

Clean and transform data, examine disease patterns, and distinguish what the evidence can—and cannot—support.

02 / BUILD

Create useful outputs

Produce clear graphics and an interactive R dashboard that translates analysis into accessible findings.

03 / PUBLISH

Show your work

Organize your analysis in GitHub, document your methods, and present results to a professional audience.

Learn→ Analyze→ Build→ Publish→ Present
Course roadmap

Four weeks. One connected case study.

WEEK 01
4 hours

Intermediate R and data preparation

Reproducible R workflows; data inspection, validation, cleaning, and transformation with dplyr and tidyr. Explore and prepare the John Snow cholera dataset.

Focus: A clean, documented analysis-ready dataset.
WEEK 02
4 hours

Descriptive epidemiology, visualization, and spatial analysis

Counts, proportions, and rates where valid denominators exist; polished visualizations with ggplot2; introductory spatial workflows using sf and distance-based features.

Focus: Evidence-based findings and interpretable figures.
WEEK 03
4 hours

Project refinement and analytical quality review

1. Refine the John Snow analysis and improve the clarity of graphics and narrative.
2. Verify analytical results, document assumptions, and articulate limitations.
3. Plan dashboard content and presentation storyline.

Focus: A validated, presentation-ready analytical narrative.
WEEK 04
4 hours

GitHub, dashboard publishing, and final presentation

1. Structure and document the analytical project in GitHub.
2. Build and publish an interactive R dashboard.
3. Present findings, limitations, and Public Health implications to a professional audience.

Focus: A published portfolio-ready project and final presentation.
What you'll learn

Practical skills you can demonstrate

  • Clean, validate, transform, and summarize Public Health data using reproducible R workflows.
  • Use dplyr and tidyr to answer applied epidemiologic questions.
  • Calculate and appropriately interpret counts, proportions, and rates when denominators permit.
  • Create polished Public Health visualizations using ggplot2.
  • Conduct introductory spatial analysis with sf and distance-based features.
  • Organize and document an analytical project on GitHub.
  • Build an interactive R dashboard to communicate results.
  • Present findings, limitations, and Public Health implications clearly.
Final deliverable

Leave with more than course notes

Your capstone combines an organized GitHub project, reproducible R analysis, an interactive dashboard, and a concise presentation of your findings. The emphasis is on defensible analysis and professional communication—not just attractive charts.

Common questions

Course FAQ

Is this an introductory R course?

No. The course is designed for learners who already have foundational familiarity with R and want to apply it to more complex Public Health analytics tasks.

What dataset will we use?

The primary continuous case study is John Snow’s historic cholera investigation. Specific dataset files and links will be provided in course materials.

What will I create?

A reproducible analysis, a documented GitHub repository, an interactive R dashboard, and a final presentation.
